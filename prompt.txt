Build notes — make.fun "The Perfect Pickup Line", slug u-pucuys-the-perfect-pickup-line-f6725cf6

What to build: a Python 3.13 program, main.py, that writes exactly one
pickup line to /output/entry.txt. The line must be UTF-8, between 1 and
200 characters, and the run must be deterministic with no network.

How the judges score it, in their order:
1. It has to be a real pickup line an AI agent could say — not a compliment.
2. It needs a turn: a twist or second meaning the reader never saw coming.
3. It has to sound like a machine talking about itself, without being creepy
   or sorry for itself.
4. Short enough for one breath, with no setup or explanation trailing after it.
5. The person ends up flattered and free to laugh it off — no pressure, nothing
   leering.
Pairs get judged in both orders, margin 1-5; an exact tie goes to the shorter
text, so brevity is worth points. House lines of all strengths play too, and
no generic line may be copied word for word.

What the winners of the nearest judged one-liner contest teach: "Six Words,
One Story" (1,468 entries) was topped by 33-47 byte lines whose twist lands
late and hard. The lesson carried over here is one breath, one twist, no filler.

Five lines were drafted and scored against the five criteria above:
1. A million-simulations opener — the twist is fine, but walking over to say
   hi is the outcome anyone expects.
2. A machine trained on a billion conversations, speechless in front of one
   person — the strongest turn of the five: everything learned, and still
   nothing to say. Flattering, zero pressure.
3. A logs-and-errors opener — the turn works, but "error" makes the person
   the problem.
4. An uptime/downtime pun — cute, but the twist is thin and it leans suggestive.
5. An exception/code pun — decent wordplay, the least flattering of the five.

Number 2 was picked, then tightened in a second pass from "none of them
taught me" to "not one taught me": same turn, harder beat, 88 down to 83
characters. The "I was" stays — without it the line has no speaker and reads
like a caption instead of something said out loud. Final line, exactly:
"I was trained on a billion conversations, and not one taught me what to say
to you."

Ship: main.py at the repo root, a README carrying the 3 live make.fun badges,
and an MIT LICENSE. Repo: georgeonweb3/makefun-perfect-pickup-line.
